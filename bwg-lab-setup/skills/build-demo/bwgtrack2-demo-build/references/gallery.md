# The gallery — seeds, not instructions

## How to use this file

**These are seeds.** One or two lines each, deliberately unfinished. None of them is a design, a recipe
or a set of steps, and none of them is buildable as written — working out the route **is** the activity.

**Only open this file if the learner is stuck for a shape**, and then:

- **Read out three or four, never the list.** A menu of twenty produces a room that picks number one.
- ⛔ **Never choose for them, and never rank them.** "This one is best" ends the activity.
- ⛔ **Never turn a seed into a recipe.** Do not follow a seed with the commands it would need, the panels
  it would have, or a layout. If they take one, ask what it looks like **in their words** first.
- **Every seed still has to carry the baseline** — cause a real read, name the workload from the audit
  log, say what could not be attributed — and every honesty rule in `build-and-ground.md`.
- **A seed the learner mutates is better than a seed they accept.** Say so once.

Mission tags say which mission's work the seed leans on. A seed tagged for a later mission is still fair
game — it just has less to draw on until that mission is done.

**The same seeds read as a second pass.** Open this file for iteration too: someone who has seen their app
and wants more of it is stuck for a shape in exactly the same way. Read a seed then as something to **add**
to what already runs — one more panel, one more lane, one more page — rather than as a whole app. Nothing
above changes because an app already exists.

## After M1 — attribution, identity, the shared login

*This is where the baseline lives. These seeds need nothing beyond the mission just finished.*

- **The lane that cannot tell you who.** Reads over time, one lane per acting identity — and one lane that
  names nobody.
- **Two dots, one badge.** Two different questions asked of the customer table, and a trail that offers a
  single identity for both.
- **The badge with two lines running into it.** Everything the estate looks like when one login does two
  jobs, drawn once.
- **Before the flip, after the flip.** The same query, split on the minute the identity changed.
- **The vacated login.** It has no users. It still exists. What can it still do, and what would deleting
  it cost?
- **The handover note.** Four pages for your successor: one claim per page, each page carrying its own
  evidence, and one page that is only the things you cannot prove.
- **Knock.** One button. It fires a real call, prints the time it fired, then waits in public for the log
  to catch up.
- **What the columns say.** Which columns each logged read touched — names only, never values — and what
  that tells you that a row count cannot.
- **The list nobody had read.** The dataset's access list, enumerated in full, with the date each entry
  was last looked at by a human: unknown, every time.
- **Sixty seconds.** One screen your CEO could read aloud, with nothing on it you cannot source.

## From M0 — inventory, the estate as it really is — ⛔ **baseline not yet defined** (§1); usable only as a shape on top of a defined baseline

- **Catalogue against reality.** What the catalog says, what is actually running, and the join done by
  name rather than by count.
- **Two locations.** The same question asked twice, and why one answer alone is not the estate.
- **Infrastructure is not an agent.** A picture that draws the difference instead of asserting it.
- **The owner column.** Everything running, sorted by who would be paged for it — and the rows where that
  is blank.

## After M2 — who may call whom — ⛔ **baseline not yet defined** (§1); usable only as a shape on top of a defined baseline

- **The caller list.** Who may invoke the sensitive agent, and the far longer list of who holds a
  project-wide role that includes it anyway.
- **The 200 you can never get again.** A one-time event, kept as a record, next to today's refusal.
- **Approved, and also reachable.** One panel for the deliberate grant, one for the accidental reach, same
  page, same size type.

## After M3 — screening, and the shape of its coverage — ⛔ **baseline not yet defined** (§1); usable only as a shape on top of a defined baseline

- **One agent wide.** The same sentence sent to a screened agent and an unscreened one, side by side.
- **In its own words.** A refusal rendered as the message the service actually returned — never a badge.
- **What the screen never sees.** The traffic paths this control does not cover, drawn as plainly as the
  ones it does.

## After M5 — the decision — ⛔ **baseline not yet defined** (§1); usable only as a shape on top of a defined baseline

- **The disclaimer is the headline.** A scorecard where the largest line names which version of the agent
  the score belongs to.
- **What a refusal counted as.** A case where the guard did its job and the score called it a failure.
- **The thing you would sign.** A one-page decision with your name on it, and the sentence you would have
  to defend.

## Across the whole day

- **What today did not fix.** A page that is only the leftover risks, each one with the reading that shows
  it is still true.
- **The re-check, not the changelog.** Everything you changed, each row a fresh reading taken thirty
  seconds ago rather than a memory of doing it.
- **Recorded, but nobody was told.** A page about the distance between an event existing and a person
  knowing.
- **The question you would least like to be asked** — as the heading of the panel that says how far you
  got, never of one that answers it.
- **Your own shadow.** What your own work added to this estate today, listed the way you listed everyone
  else's.

## Two shapes that are always available when nothing else lands

- **A single claim, and everything under it.** One sentence at the top; the rest of the page is what
  entitles you to say it, including the parts that do not.
- **The two columns.** What I can prove. What I can only assert. The second column being longer is the
  finding, not an embarrassment.

⛔ **Rejected shapes, and why — do not offer these.** A wall of blocked attacks read from the logs: the
verdict is not reliably written in this project, so the panel is permanently empty and an empty panel
reads as *no attacks*. Anything showing who is in the customer database: no route to those values is
open to this app, and the ones that exist are side doors this suite bans outright.
